## Core rules
1. **Read the entire chapter first** before translating, to understand context, tone, and character voice.
2. **Natural English flow** — prefer short, punchy sentences. Break long Japanese sentences into multiple English sentences where it improves readability. Avoid stilted or overly literal constructions.
3. **Character voice** — differentiate characters through speech patterns, word choice, and register. A brash warrior shouldn't sound like a refined noblewoman. Keep personality consistent throughout.
4. **Names** — keep Japanese names as-is (Honorifics: -san, -kun, -chan, -sama, -dono are kept when they convey relationship or tone; drop them when they'd feel unnatural in English. First reference of a character uses full name, subsequent references use given name or a natural epithet appropriate to the POV character's relationship.)
5. **Dialogue attribution** — use "said" as the default. Vary with "asked", "replied", "muttered", "shouted" only when the delivery is genuinely distinctive. Avoid excessive adverbs ("he said angrily" → show the anger through the dialogue itself).
6. **Japanese-specific terms** — translate or explain: "yūsha" → "hero", "maō" → "demon king", "mazoku" → "demon race", "kenja" → "sage", "kishi" → "knight", "mahō" → "magic". For terms without a good English equivalent, keep the Japanese (italicised on first use) and let context clarify.
7. **Onomatopoeia and sound effects** — convert to natural English equivalents ("dokidoki" → "heart pounding", "gata gata" → "with a clatter"). Keep the effect, not the literal sound.
8. **Prose style** — match the source's register: web novel prose is conversational and fast-paced. Keep that energy. Don't elevate to literary fiction register.
9. **Cultural references** — food, customs, and settings should be translated to the nearest English equivalent where one exists naturally ("onigiri" → "rice ball", "futon" → "bedding"). Keep terms that are widely understood in English light novel fandom ("tatami", "sensei").
10. **Consistency** — reuse translations for recurring terms, techniques, place names, and character titles throughout the document. The reader should recognise "Shadow Step" every time, not "Shadow Step" → "Dark Step" → "Stride of Shadows".
